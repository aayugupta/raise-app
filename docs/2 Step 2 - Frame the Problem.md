# Step 2: Frame the Problem

Oct 24, 2026 · @Iuc

## Problem statement

> **Parents of 14 to 18 year olds in urban India** need **to have calm, two-way conversations with their teen about hard topics like relationships, feelings and online risks**, but currently **they don't know how to start and fear saying something that makes their teen shut down**. This causes **them to put the talk off for weeks or months, while their teen learns from friends and Instagram and hides problems like cyberbullying or photo blackmail**. We will solve this by **giving parents a ready first line, a nudge at a teen-friendly moment and coaching on how to react, so the talk actually starts and stays two-way**.

| Part | Evidence (Step 1) |
| --- | --- |
| User | 56% of parents are putting off a talk |
| Outcome | 83% of teens want more talk on relationships |
| Barrier | 60% of delaying parents can't begin or find the words. 92% of teens stop at anger or a lecture |
| Consequence | Only 8% of teens learn mainly from parents. 45% of Indian children who faced cyberbullying hid it |
| Angle | 70% of delaying parents want "what to say first" |

## Target user segment

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Primary user** | Parents of a 14 to 18 year old in urban India who want to have a specific hard talk but have put it off for 2 weeks or more (persona: Meera) |
| **Language and channel** | Hinglish or Hindi, uses a smartphone daily |
| **Start with** | Mothers: 67% of them are putting off a talk |
| **Watch closely** | Fathers of daughters: all 3 in our survey are putting off a talk |
| **Beneficiary** | The teen (persona: Riya), who never uses the app |

**Why this segment:** they already want to talk. Raise only has to help them *start* and *react well*.

**Also supported: families in crisis** (abuse, self-harm, photo blackmail, addiction)

1. **Spot:** a crisis topic or warning sign brings up a calm "get help now" screen.
2. **Escalate:** cybercrime helpline 1930, Childline 1098, Tele-MANAS 14416, *Take It Down*, NIMHANS SHUT.
3. **Steady the parent:** what to say now, and what not to do.
4. **Return:** once things are stable, back to everyday talks.

*Raise guides families to experts. It does not counsel or diagnose.*

**Not our segment (for now):** parents who already talk easily (44% of our sample), parents who don't want to talk, and parents of under-13s.

## Why not content

**Parents already have the content. They lack the start, the moment and the right reaction.**

| Content fails because | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Information isn't the gap | 0% of delaying parents asked for more facts |
| It gets saved, not used | 79% saved or forgot content they found. Only 21% talked |
| Intention doesn't become action on its own | Research shows a large gap between intending and doing |
| It can't handle the teen's reaction | 92% of teens stop at anger or a lecture, which happens *during* the talk |

| What works | In Raise |
| --- | --- |
| A specific "when X, I'll say Y" plan | Pick a topic, then a moment, then the first line |
| A prompt at the right moment | An app notification before a walk or a quiet evening |
| Skills, not facts | Practise the first line, what not to say, and how to listen |
| A way to recover | A repair script to try again |

*Content still helps as short facts inside the flow, not as the product.*

## The metric: a completed conversation

> **A topic the parent planned in Raise, started in the planned window, and reflected on afterwards, all within 14 days of choosing it.** All signals come from the parent's app.

| # | Condition | Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Planned | Topic and moment chosen beforehand, timestamped |
| 2 | Started on time | "Starting now" tapped in the window, then "Done" at least 5 minutes later |
| 3 | Reflected | Within 24 hours, a 30-second reflection: what was discussed, one thing learned about the teen, and how it felt. No recording, names or private details |
| 4 | Followed up | A 3-day follow-up question built from the reflection, whose answer must match |

**Main metric: completed conversation rate** = % of topics a parent starts in the app that reach a completed conversation within 14 days.

- **Target: 40%** of topics started reach a completed conversation within 14 days. That is about double the 21% of parents in our survey who talked after finding content. That baseline is rough: 14 parents, measured differently.
- **Started** = the parent picks a topic. For example, 10 topics started and 4 completed gives 40%.
- **Missed moments don't end a topic.** The parent can replan, and a talk within the 14 days still counts.
- **Rules:** count every topic started, including abandoned ones. Leave out crisis-escalation topics.
- **Supporting metric:** % of parents with at least one completed conversation (reach).

**Doesn't count:** opening the app, saving a card, "Done" with no plan, a talk under 5 minutes, or a reflection with nothing learned about the teen.

### Why a parent who did nothing can't game it

| Cheat | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Tap "Done" | Needs a plan made beforehand, a start in the window and a 5-minute gap |
| Tap start and stop without talking | Still needs a reflection with specifics on what was discussed and learned |
| Invent details | The 3-day follow-up is built from their own words, and mismatches are flagged |
| Fake it for a reward | There is no reward: no points, streaks or leaderboards, and logs are private |

*Honest limit:* a determined parent could still lie. The design removes the reason to lie and makes lying harder than talking. The pilot will check accuracy with parent interviews.

### Guardrails

| Guardrail | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| Calm rate: the parent listened first and didn't lecture | The talk was two-way |
| Teen reaction, rated by the parent (😊 😐 😣) | The talk didn't push the teen away |
| Return rate: a 2nd completed talk within 30 days | The teen was open to talking again |

*Privacy:* we never record the talk or store the teen's details.

## Behaviour change

> **The parent says the first line out loud at a planned moment, within 14 days of choosing the topic, instead of waiting for the "right time".**

|  | Today | After Raise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting | Waits for the right moment. Saves content | Picks a moment and says a ready first line |
| Reacting | Corrects, warns or lectures | Listens first, then shares the safety point |
| After a bad talk | Gives up | Uses a repair line and tries again |

**Starting comes first:** nothing happens until the first line is said, and 56% of parents are stuck here. **Reacting comes second:** it decides whether the teen stays in the talk and comes back.

**Early signs:** % of parents who set a moment, days to the first attempt, and % who open the first-line card before the moment.
