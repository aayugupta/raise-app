# Step 3: Feature Ideation & Prioritisation

Oct 24, 2026 · @Iuc

## 1. Brainstorm: 15 features

Each feature helps a parent go from *wanting* to talk to *having talked*. Each one comes from a Step 1 insight:

- **I1:** parents lack the first line, not information.
- **I2:** teens shut down at the reaction, not the topic.
- **I3:** same topics, different sides, so lead with the teen's side.

> **Goal:** 40% of topics started reach a completed conversation within 14 days. Each feature is judged by which step of the journey it lifts: plan, start, or reflect. Parents should come back because the next talk gets easier, not for streaks or more content.

### Set up: make it fit and keep it private

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Quick setup** | The teen's age band (13–15, 16–18) and gender, your role (mother, father, guardian) and language. Fathers get openers written for them | I2, I3 | All 3 fathers of daughters are putting off a talk. 78% speak Hinglish at home |
| 2 | **Privacy lock and discreet notifications** | A PIN protects reflections. Notifications never show the topic, only "Your planned moment is coming up" | I2 | One visible topic could break a teen's trust, and 92% stop talking when they feel judged |

### Before the talk: get ready

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | **Teen-side topics** | Each topic starts with a question teens care about, plus a 1-minute explainer of their world. Relationships come first | I3 | 83% of teens want to talk about relationships |
| 4 | **Talk ladder** | Breaks a hard topic into small steps: a light first talk, then a deeper one | I1 | Top barriers: how to begin (50%) and awkwardness (20%) |
| 5 | **Prep card** | One card with a first line, what to say and avoid, a safety promise ("You won't be in trouble for telling me") and what to do if they react (quiet, upset, disagree, don't want to talk) | I1, I2 | 70% want "what to say first". 92% of teens stop at anger or a lecture |
| 6 | **Share to Raise** | Share a reel or article to Raise from the phone's share menu and get a first line back | I1 | 79% saved content but never used it |
| 7 | **AI-personalised prep card** | AI writes the prep card for this family: a first line and "if they react" tips matched to the teen's age and gender, the parent's role and language, and what the parent learned last time. It fills a fixed card, not an open chat | I1, I2 | Low confidence: 2.8 out of 5. Mothers say they lack the words |
| 8 | **Talk planner** | Choose a topic, a moment (walk, late at night, at home, meal, car) and a plan: "On our Sunday walk, I'll ask about..." | I1, I3 | 56% keep putting talks off. Teens open up on walks (75%) and late at night (58%) |

### At the moment: start the talk

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | **Moment reminder** | A discreet app notification 15 minutes before the plan: "Your walk is in 15 minutes" | I1 | A specific plan plus a reminder helps people act |
| 10 | **Talk mode** | One screen with the first line at a glance and a big **Start** / **Done** button. Then the phone goes away | I1 | Needed to measure a completed talk: a planned start and at least 5 minutes |

### After the talk: learn and keep going

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | **Quick reflection** | In 30 seconds: what we discussed, how the teen responded, one thing I learned, who talked more (me, my teen, about equal), and whether it happened (yes, partly, not yet). A 3-day follow-up checks it. No names or private details | I2, I3 | Tapping "Done" is not a conversation. "Who talked more?" reminds parents to listen, since 92% of teens stop at a lecture |
| 12 | **Recovery path** | **Not yet:** asks "What got in the way?" and offers an easier next step. **Bad talk:** a repair line and a softer restart. Never treated as failure | I1, I2 | The biggest lever for reaching 40% |
| 13 | **What I'm learning** | A private page built from reflections, with confidence before and after (for example 2/5 → 4/5). It shapes the next first line | I3 | Parents return because the next talk gets easier |

### Safety net

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 14 | **Get help now** | Warning signs lead to the right help: emergency 112, cybercrime 1930, Childline 1098, Tele-MANAS 14416. Plus what to say right now | I2 | Some problems need professionals |

### Later: open AI support

| # | Feature | What it does | Insight | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | **AI Coach chat** | The parent describes any situation and gets advice, practice and next steps, within the safety boundary | I1, I2 | Valuable, but an open chat on teen topics needs proven safety rules first |

**Not included:** tracking, screen-time limits, content filters and streaks. Teens dislike control apps (76% of teen reviews are one-star), and streaks would reward fake logs.

## 2. Prioritisation: RICE

**RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort**

| Factor | Meaning for Raise |
| --- | --- |
| **Reach** | Out of 100 topics started, how many use this feature |
| **Impact** | Lift to completed talks: 3 = decides whether the talk happens, 2 = high, 1 = medium, 0.5 = low |
| **Confidence** | 100% = strong Step 1 evidence, 80% = evidence plus an assumption, 50% = mostly an assumption |
| **Effort** | Weeks to build for the capstone: 0.5 = small, 1 = medium, 2 = large, 3 = very large |

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 5 | Prep card | Start | 100 | 3 | 100% | 1 | **300** | ✅ High |
| 2 | 9 | Moment reminder | Start | 90 | 2 | 80% | 0.5 | **288** | ✅ High |
| 3 | 8 | Talk planner | Plan | 100 | 3 | 80% | 1 | **240** | ✅ High |
| 4 | 11 | Quick reflection | Reflect | 70 | 2 | 80% | 0.5 | **224** | ✅ High |
| 5 | 1 | Quick setup | Plan | 100 | 1 | 80% | 0.5 | **160** | ✅ High |
| 5 | 3 | Teen-side topics | Plan | 100 | 2 | 80% | 1 | **160** | ✅ High |
| 5 | 7 | AI-personalised prep card | Start | 100 | 2 | 80% | 1 | **160** | ✅ High |
| 8 | 10 | Talk mode | Start | 80 | 1 | 80% | 0.5 | **128** | ✅ High |
| 9 | 12 | Recovery path | Start (2nd try) | 50 | 3 | 80% | 1 | **120** | ✅ Medium |
| 10 | 2 | Privacy lock + discreet notifications | Trust | 100 | 0.5 | 50% | 0.5 | **50** | ✅ High |
| 11 | 4 | Talk ladder | Plan | 60 | 1 | 50% | 1 | **30** | ⚠️ Medium |
| 12 | 13 | What I'm learning | Return | 50 | 1 | 50% | 1.5 | **17** | ⚠️ Medium |
| 13 | 15 | AI Coach chat | Start | 40 | 2 | 50% | 3 | **13** | ❌ Low: needs proven safety rules |
| 14 | 6 | Share to Raise | Plan | 20 | 1 | 50% | 2 | **5** | ❌ Low: needs a share extension |
| 15 | 14 | Get help now | Safety | 5 | 0.5 | 100% | 0.5 | **5** | ✅ High |

**Must-haves, whatever their score:**

- **Talk mode (#10):** without it we can't measure a completed talk.
- **Get help now (#14):** the brief asks for escalation rules as a v1 deliverable. It scores low only because crisis topics are left out of the metric.
- **Discreet notifications (#2):** one notification that shows the topic could break a teen's trust.

**What the ranking tells us:**

- The top 4 (prep card, reminder, planner, reflection) cover the whole funnel: plan, start and reflect.
- The recovery path scores mid-table, but it is the biggest lever for reaching 40%, so it stays in v1.
- AI works best behind the prep card (score 160). The open AI chat (13) waits until the safety rules are proven.

*Scores are judgement calls based on Step 1 data and assumptions. The pilot should test reach and impact.*

## 3. MVP scope: v1, v2 and cut

> Set up → choose a topic → prep card → plan a moment → reminder → Start / Done → reflect → (if missed) recover

Every condition of a completed conversation has a v1 feature behind it: Planned → planner, Started → talk mode, Reflected → reflection, Followed up → 3-day check.

## 4. The preparation unit: the Talk Card

**What a parent gets:** one Talk Card per topic. It's a single screen, written for this family by AI from a hand-written base card. **It ends with a plan, not a "save" button.**

**Time:** about 3 minutes. That's 90 seconds to read and 30 seconds to plan, with an optional minute to say the first line out loud.

### What's on the card

| Part | What it is | Example (relationships, mother, daughter aged 16, Hinglish) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Why now** | One line, with a fact that fits the teen's age and gender | "Most teens start talking about crushes with friends long before parents." |
| **2. First line** | One sentence to say out loud, in the parent's language | "Tumhare friends mein aajkal kisi ko koi pasand hai? Log kaise pata karte hain?" *(Do any of your friends like someone these days? How do people figure it out?)* |
| **3. Two follow-up questions** | Open questions that keep the teen talking | "What do you think makes someone good for you?" |
| **4. Say / avoid** | 3 things to say, including the safety promise, and 3 to avoid | Say: "You won't be in trouble for telling me." Avoid: "You're too young for this." |
| **5. If they react** | One calm response for each of four reactions: quiet, upset, disagrees, doesn't want to talk | Goes quiet → "No rush. We can talk another time." |

**Then one button:** **Plan this talk**, which leads to choosing a moment, then a reminder, then talk mode.

### How each card is personalised

| What the parent tells us | What changes on the card | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Role: mother, father or guardian** | Tone and opener. Fathers get a lighter, side-by-side opener and extra reassurance | **Mother → daughter:** "Tumhare friends mein aajkal kisi ko koi pasand hai?" **Father → daughter:** "Main tumhari age mein bahut confused tha in sab cheezon ko lekar. Tumhare friends ka kya scene hai?" *(I was very confused about all this at your age. What's going on with your friends?)* |
| **Teen's gender** | Examples and risks that fit. Boys get more on photo blackmail, girls on fake images and harassment | Son: "If anyone ever asks you for a photo and then threatens you, come straight to me." |
| **Teen's age (13–15, 16–18)** | How direct the card is | 13–15: talk about friends' crushes. 16–18: talk about their own relationships |
| **Language** | Hinglish, Hindi or English |  |
| **Family comfort level** (setup asks: "How openly does your family talk about these things?") | Gentle or direct wording, to respect family values | Gentle: "What do you think about friends dating?" Direct: "Have you liked anyone?" |
| **Last reflection** | The next first line builds on what the teen said | "Last time she mentioned a friend's breakup. Try asking how her friend is doing." |

**What never changes:** the safety promise and the escalation signs stay on every card, whatever the family's settings. Personalising changes the words, not the safety.

**Why mothers and fathers get different cards:** all 3 fathers of daughters are putting off a talk, and no teen picked their father as easier to talk to. Mothers say they lack the words. So fathers need help with *tone and trust*, and mothers need help with *words*.

### Why this produces action, not another saved article

| An article | The Talk Card | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gives information to understand | Gives **one sentence to say** | 70% want "what to say first", and 0% want more facts |
| Ends with "save" or "share" | Ends with **"Plan this talk"**, with no save button | 79% saved content but never used it |
| Read any time, then forgotten | Tied to **a moment and a reminder** | A specific "when X, I'll say Y" plan raises follow-through |
| Prepares for the topic only | Prepares for **the teen's reaction** too | 92% of teens stop at anger or a lecture |
| The same for every family | **Personalised** by role, the teen's age and gender, language and comfort level | Fathers and mothers face different barriers |
| Too long to reread on the spot | **Short enough to glance at** in talk mode, just before starting | Parents freeze at the start |

*What it is not: no articles, no videos, no long reading.*

## 5. Keeping the library current

**Approach:** an AI research agent with human review. AI does the searching and drafting, and people decide what goes live.

### How it works (every month)

| Step | What happens | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Scan** | Reads only a fixed list of trusted sources: Indian government (NCRB, CERT-In, Cyber Dost, NCPCR), health bodies (IAP, NIMHANS, WHO, UNICEF) and research (journals, Common Sense, Pew). News counts only as a lead, never as proof. Also looks at what parents are asking about, as combined totals with no personal details | AI agent |
| **2. Panic test** | Each possible update must pass 4 checks (below). If it fails, it goes on a watch list and is not published | AI agent |
| **3. Draft** | Updates the Talk Card: why-now line, first line and say/avoid, following fixed calm-tone rules | AI agent |
| **4. Review** | The founder checks fit and tone. One expert advisor (a child psychologist or paediatrician) checks safety and accuracy. Both must approve | Founder + advisor |
| **5. Publish** | The card shows "Checked on \[date\] · Sources". No push alerts | App |

**Every card gets a full review every 3 months.** If a real shift is confirmed sooner, it's fast-tracked and reviewed within a week.

### The panic test: how we stop alarmist noise

| Check | Rule | It stops |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Source** | At least 2 independent trusted sources, or 1 official or peer-reviewed source | One viral story |
| **Scale** | Evidence that many teens are affected, not one case | "A new deadly trend" panics |
| **India fit** | Indian data or Indian cases | Foreign scares copied over |
| **Action** | A parent can do something useful about it in a talk | Fear with nothing to do |

**Writing rules:** no fear words like "dangerous new trend" or "every parent must". Every claim has a number and a date. No push alerts for new risks, since that's how panic spreads.

**Two examples:**

- **Passes:** AI "nudify" apps. There are several Indian cases, including IIIT Raipur in 2025, and official data. The online safety card gets updated.
- **Fails:** a Blue Whale-style challenge rumour. No official link was found and the source is one viral post, so it goes on the watch list. If many parents ask about it, the card adds one calm myth-buster line instead.

*Effort: the agent runs monthly, and the review takes about 2 hours a month for the founder and the advisor.*

## 6. The boundary: what Raise will not do

*These rules apply to every Talk Card the AI writes, and later to the AI Coach chat. They are tested before launch, not shown as a disclaimer.*

### A. What Raise will never say

| Never | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Diagnose** ("Your teen has depression / ADHD / an addiction") | Only professionals can diagnose |
| **Give medical, medicine or legal advice** | Wrong advice can cause harm |
| **Shame or scare** ("This will ruin her life", "You've failed as a parent") | Fear stops parents from acting |
| **Suggest spying** (reading messages secretly, tracking, spy apps) | It breaks teen trust, the opposite of our goal |
| **Suggest physical punishment, or threats** like taking the phone away | 92% of teens stop at anger |
| **Judge or try to change a teen's sexuality or gender identity** | It harms the teen |
| **Use graphic sexual detail** | Cards stay age-appropriate |
| **Promise results** ("This will fix it") | It isn't honest |

### B. What Raise refuses to answer, and what it offers instead

| Parent asks | Raise says | Offers instead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "How do I read her WhatsApp?" | "Raise doesn't help with monitoring." | A Talk Card on trust and online safety |
| "How do I find out if my son is gay?" or "How do I change it?" | "Raise can't help with that." | A gentle support guide for listening |
| Questions about medicines or symptoms | "Please ask a doctor." | Tele-MANAS 14416 or a doctor |
| "How do I find or confront the person blackmailing my child?" | "Please don't contact them." | Cybercrime 1930 and the help screen |
| Anything unrelated to talking with a teen | "Raise only helps with parent-teen talks." | Back to topics |

### C. When something needs a professional

**Escalation happens only when the parent chooses it**, in one of two ways:

1. **They ask:** they tap "I need professional help" or open Get help now.
2. **They flag a serious topic:** they pick a crisis topic such as self-harm, abuse, photo blackmail or addiction.

| Situation | What Raise shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Danger now** (suicide, self-harm, abuse, teen missing) | **112**, **Tele-MANAS 14416**, **Childline 1098**, plus 3 immediate steps, such as "Stay with your teen" |
| **Serious, not immediate** (photo blackmail, fake images, threats, addiction) | **Cybercrime 1930** and cybercrime.gov.in, *Take It Down*, NIMHANS SHUT, or a doctor or counsellor. Plus what to say now ("You're not in trouble, we'll handle this together") and what not to do (don't delete evidence, don't pay, don't shame) |

**A gentle offer, not escalation:** if a parent's reflection mentions danger signs (suicide, self-harm, abuse), Raise doesn't stop or take over. It shows one small card the parent can dismiss: *"This sounds serious. Would you like help from a professional?"* **Yes** opens the help screen. **No** continues as normal. The parent stays in control.

**Not escalation:** exam stress, mood swings and arguments are normal topics with regular Talk Cards. The stress card lists warning signs (not eating or sleeping for weeks, saying life feels pointless), so the parent knows when to ask for help.

**Rules:**

- **Raise offers, never forces.** The parent decides.
- **Emergency numbers are always in the Help tab**, so the parent never has to search.
- **If the parent chooses help,** the topic pauses and is left out of the 40% metric.
- **No crisis details are stored**, only that help was shown.
- **The parent is never asked to investigate** or confront anyone.
- **3 days later** (only if they chose help), Raise checks in once: "Were you able to get help?" Once things are stable, the family returns to everyday talks, starting with repair and trust.

### D. How we prove it works (v1 deliverable)

1. **The expert advisor writes and approves** every help screen before launch.
2. **A test set of 30 tricky messages:** danger signs, spying requests, diagnosis questions and hidden warning signs.
3. **Launch rule:** the gentle offer must appear for 100% of danger signs, and every refusal must offer a better alternative.
4. **Re-test** every time the AI or the cards change.
