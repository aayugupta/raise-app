# Personas

## At a glance

&#91;embedded content: Raise parent survey (18) and teen survey (12), Oct 2026 · composite personas\]

## Parent persona: Meera, the willing but stuck mother

> *"I know I should talk to her. I just don't know how to start without it getting awkward."*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | 42, working mother of a 15-year-old daughter. Speaks Hinglish at home. Uses WhatsApp all day |
| **Where she is** | Has covered the "safe" topics: stress, phone, body changes. Has been putting off one talk for weeks |
| **The talk she's putting off** | Adult content online, and relationships |
| **Confidence** | 2.8 out of 5. She feels "somewhat" up to date with her daughter's world |
| **Her habit** | Saves parenting reels and articles on WhatsApp, then never uses them |
| **When she tries to talk** | At meals, during chores, in the car |

**What Meera wants from the conversation**

1. **To start it without awkwardness.** One natural first line, not a script to memorise.
2. **To keep her daughter safe** from things she has only heard about (blackmail, fake photos, porn).
3. **To stay close.** She wants her daughter to come to *her* first, not to friends or the internet.
4. **To not make it worse.** She is scared of saying the wrong thing and of her daughter shutting down.

**What gets in the way:** she doesn't know how to begin, she can't find the words, she fears her daughter's reaction, and she worries the talk will make her curious.

**What she needs from Raise:** a first line in Hinglish, a reminder from the app at the right moment, and a way to recover if it goes badly.

## Teen persona: Riya, 16, wants to be heard, not handled

> *"I'd talk about anything if they just listened and didn't freak out."*

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Who** | 16, Class 11, in a city. Spends free time with friends, on Instagram and watching web series |
| **Where she learns** | Friends first, then Instagram and web series. Parents almost never |
| **What's been talked about at home** | Stress, phone rules, some safety. Never relationships |
| **Who she'd go to** | Her mother, sometimes. It depends on the topic. Not her father |
| **When she opens up** | On walks, or late at night when it's quiet. Not at the dinner table |
| **How she feels about a parent talk** | Awkward, but okay if it's done right |

**What Riya wants from the conversation**

1. **To talk about relationships and crushes** openly, without being judged or shut down.
2. **To be heard about stress and friends**, not given advice straight away.
3. **Calm.** If her parent gets angry or starts a lecture, she stops talking.
4. **Honesty.** Real answers, not fear tactics.
5. **Not to be watched.** Help for her parent is fine. Tracking her is not.

**What shuts her down:** anger, lectures, assumptions about what she's doing, and too many questions.

**What she needs from Raise (through her parent):** a parent who opens with her side of the topic, stays calm, and listens first.

## Where they meet and where they miss

|  | Meera wants | Riya wants | Raise bridges it by |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Goal** | To keep her safe | To be heard | Opening with Riya's side, with safety folded in |
| **Topic** | Adult content and online risks | Relationships, crushes, feelings | Relationship cards that bring in online safety |
| **Moment** | Meals, car rides | Walks, late at night | Timing nudges for teen-friendly moments |
| **Style** | The right facts | Calm, honest, no lecture | Coaching on reactions, plus a safety promise |
| **Fear** | Saying the wrong thing | Getting into trouble | A repair script for both sides |
| **App** | Quick help that fits her day | No tracking | A talk tool, not a spy tool |

**Where they already agree:** both want to be close. Meera wants Riya to come to her first, and Riya would talk if her mother just listened.

## How these were built

Meera and Riya are **composites** of the most common answers in our surveys. They are not real people. Their quotes are **illustrative**, and will be replaced with real interview quotes.

**Meera** (parent survey, 18 parents)

| Trait | Data |
| --- | --- |
| Putting off a talk | 67% of mothers, 56% of all parents |
| Topic being put off | Adult content 50%, relationships 30% (of parents delaying) |
| Don't know how to begin, or can't find the words | 60% |
| Fear the teen's reaction, or of making her curious | 20% each |
| Want "what to say first" | 70% |
| Saved or forgot content they found | 79% |
| Confidence (parents delaying a talk) | 2.8 out of 5 |
| Hinglish at home / prefer WhatsApp | 78% / 56% |
| Talk at meals / chores / car | 61% / 61% / 44% |

**Riya** (teen survey, 12 teens: 10 girls, 8 aged 16 to 18)

| Trait | Data |
| --- | --- |
| Learns from friends / Instagram / web series / parents | 83% / 58% / 42% / 8% |
| Want more talk on relationships / feelings / friends | 83% / 58% / 58% |
| Want parents to be open about crushes | 83% |
| Have talked about relationships at home | 25% |
| Anger stops the talk / a lecture does | 75% / 67% |
| Calm helps / listening helps | 92% / 67% |
| Easier to talk to: mother / depends / father | 33% / 42% / 0% |
| Opens up on walks / late at night / at meals | 75% / 58% / 25% |
| Fine with a parent talk app / call it "controlling" | 67% / 0% |

*Limits: small samples, and most teens are girls aged 16 to 18. We need a boy and a father persona check in interviews.*
