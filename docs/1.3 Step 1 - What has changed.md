# What has changed: real shifts vs moral panic

## Summary

> **Key finding:** parents are behind on *where* teens live and learn (friends, Instagram, web series) and on a few really new risks. Meanwhile adults pass around panics that pull attention away from those real risks.

| Genuine shifts (parents are behind) | Moral panics (adults over-worry) |
| --- | --- |
| Teens learn from friends and Instagram, not parents | Viral "suicide games" like Blue Whale |
| Blackmail with photos (sextortion) | "Screen hours ruin kids" |
| Fake nude images made with AI | "Strangers are the main danger" |
| Early exposure to adult content | "Games make kids violent" |
| Money online: UPI and betting-style games |  |
| AI chatbots as a place to talk |  |
| Vaping |  |

**Still unclear:** how harmful social media itself is.

### New risks parents are behind on

| Shift | What's new | How far behind parents are |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Photo blackmail** | 50,000+ global reports in 2025, up about 39% from 2024. Most targets are teen boys. In India, about 89% of child cybercrime cases involve explicit material | Parents see it as a "girls' problem". 45% of Indian children who faced cyberbullying hid it from their parents |
| **Fake AI nudes** | Any normal photo can become a fake nude. In 2025 an IIIT Raipur student made fake images of about 36 classmates | "Don't send photos" no longer keeps a child safe. Only 33% of our parents have talked about consent |
| **Adult content, early** | 73% of teens have seen porn, and over half of them by age 13 | Only 43% have talked about it with a trusted adult |
| **Money online** | Teens pay by UPI. Real-money games were banned in 2025, after about ₹20,000 crore in losses a year | Money talk is still about saving, not scams or betting-style games |
| **AI chatbots** | 72% of US teens have tried AI companions, and 52% use them regularly | No Indian data yet, and 0% of our teens named AI. It is still worth watching |
| **Vaping** | Banned since 2019. Vapes look like pens or USB sticks | 96% of teens didn't know vapes are banned, and 61% had never heard anything against them, even from parents |

*Note: Indian police figures are reported cases only, so the real numbers are likely higher.*

## 2. Moral panics circulating among adults

| What adults forward | What the evidence says | Real issue to focus on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **"Blue Whale" and suicide games** | The government found no link in any reported case | Teen distress, not the game |
| **"Screen hours ruin kids"** | Screen use explains at most 0.4% of teen wellbeing. Bullying matters about 4 times more | What they do online, and sleep |
| **"Strangers are the danger"** | About 97% of POCSO offenders are known to the child | Teens must feel safe reporting *anyone* |
| **"Games make kids violent"** | There is not enough evidence | Money, sleep and chats with strangers |

**Why it matters:** panic leads parents to check phones and give warnings. That is exactly what makes teens hide things.

## 3. What doctors say

*Published guidance. We did not interview doctors.*

| Source | Key advice |
| --- | --- |
| **Indian Academy of Pediatrics** | No fixed screen limit for teens. Protect 8 to 9 hours of sleep, turn screens off an hour before bed, and set rules *together* |
| **NIMHANS SHUT clinic** | Stress and family issues drive tech addiction. Banning the phone is not the first step, because teens hide problems when they fear losing it |
| **WHO** | Gaming disorder is real, but it affects only a small share of gamers |
| **US Surgeon General** | Teens on social media more than 3 hours a day face double the risk of depression and anxiety symptoms. A caution, not proof |

**Doctors agree:** sleep, open talk and family rules work better than spying or banning.

## 4. What app reviews show

- Most "parenting apps" for teens are control apps that block and track.
- In 29,272 reviews of 66 such apps, teens rated them 1.8 out of 5 on average and parents 3.7. **76%** of teen reviews were one-star.
- Teens' complaints: too strict (35%), privacy invaded (23%), "used instead of talking to me" (14%).
- Teens did like tools that helped them manage themselves (23%).

*Note: this is a 2018 study, not from India.*

## 5. What our surveys add

| Finding | % |
| --- | --- |
| Teens who want more talk about relationships | **83%** |
| Teens who want more talk about adult content | 17% |
| Delaying parents whose delayed topic is adult content | **50%** |
| Delaying parents who don't know how to begin | 60% |
| Teens who say a lecture or anger stops the talk | **92%** |
| Teens fine with an app for parents | 67% |
| Teens who call such an app "controlling" | **0%** |

*Bases: 18 parents (10 of them delaying a talk) and 12 teens. Read these as signals, not proof.*

## 6. What this means for Raise

1. **Short risk cards** for photo blackmail, fake nudes, adult content, money and vaping. Each card says what it is, gives a first line to say, and explains what to do.
2. **A safety promise:** *"If anyone threatens you with a photo, come to me. You won't be in trouble."*
3. **A talk tool, not a spy tool.** No tracking or blocking.
4. **A myth-buster for panics.** No viral alerts. Every card shows its source and the date it was checked.
5. **Real help:** cybercrime helpline 1930, cybercrime.gov.in, *Take It Down*, NIMHANS SHUT.
6. **Review cards every 3 months**, because this world changes fast.
