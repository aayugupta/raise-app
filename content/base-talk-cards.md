# Raise — Base Talk Cards (DRAFT for Aayushi to edit)

Status: **first draft by Claude, 25 Oct 2026; questions, Say / Don't say and reactions rewritten in a warmer parent's voice on 29 Oct 2026.** Not yet reviewed by Aayushi or an expert advisor.

## How to edit this file

- Change any wording directly. Keep the headings as they are, so the app can read each part.
- Things in **[square brackets]** are notes for you. Delete or answer them.
- The **first line** is written in all three languages. Everything else is in English. The AI translates it into the parent's chosen language, and personalises it, when it writes each family's card.
- The **safety promise** and **when to get help** stay on every card. The AI is never allowed to change or remove them.

## How the AI personalises each base card

| Setup answer | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Parent's role | Fathers get a lighter, side-by-side opener and extra reassurance. Mothers get help with words |
| Teen's age band | 12–14: talk about friends and "people your age". 15–16: gently touch on the teen's own life. 17–19: can be more direct about their own life |
| Teen's gender | Examples and risks that fit (e.g. boys: photo blackmail; girls: fake images, harassment) |
| Language | Hinglish (default), Hindi or English |
| Family comfort level | Gentle or direct wording |
| What the parent wants to talk about | The "why now" and follow-ups lean towards it |

The AI only rewords. It never adds new facts, numbers, advice or help numbers. Those come only from this file.

## Card 1 — Relationships and crushes

**Topic ID:** `relationships`

**The teen's question (how the topic is shown):** "How do you know if someone is good for you?"

**Why now:**
10 of 12 teens in our survey wish their parents talked about relationships more, but only 1 of 12 learns mainly from parents. Friends and Instagram fill the gap. *(Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tumhare friends mein aajkal kisi ko koi pasand hai? Log kaise pata karte hain?"
- Hindi: "तुम्हारे दोस्तों में आजकल किसी को कोई पसंद है? लोग कैसे पता करते हैं?"
- English: "Do any of your friends like someone these days? How do people figure it out?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "Who's someone you just feel good around? What makes them like that?"
2. "Shows make love look so dramatic. What do you think they get totally wrong?"
3. "Okay, honest question: is there any song or show that actually gets crushes right?"
4. "How do people in your class even tell someone they like them these days?"
5. "What makes a friendship feel safe to you?"
6. "Ever had someone be super sweet… and then you realised they wanted something?"
7. "What do you wish I understood about how your generation sees relationships?"
8. "If a friend's relationship seemed off, what would make you notice?"
9. "What's one thing parents always get wrong about crushes?"
10. "If you ever want to ask me something about this, what's easiest: a walk, a late-night chat, or a text?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "Honestly? I had crushes at your age too. Totally normal."
3. "Real life isn't like the films. If you ever wonder what's real, just ask me. No lecture, promise."
4. "Whoever you like, you can tell me first. I'd rather hear it from you than from anyone else."
5. "Liking someone doesn't make you a bad child. It just means you're growing up."
6. "If anyone ever makes you feel uncomfortable, you can always come home and tell me."

**Avoid:**
1. "You're too young for this." (It shuts the door, and next time they won't tell you.)
2. "Who is it? Tell me their name." (It turns a chat into questioning.)
3. Teasing about crushes, even lovingly. (Teens remember the joke, not the love.)
4. "What will people say?" ("Log kya kahenge?") (They hear that the family's image matters more than they do.)
5. "Focus on your studies, all this can wait." (It tells them their feelings are a distraction, so they hide them.)
6. Telling relatives or other parents what your child shared. (One leak, and they won't confide in you again.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No rush at all. We can talk whenever. I'm around."
- **Get upset:** "Hey, I'm not here to question you. I just want you to know you can come to me."
- **Disagree:** "Fair. Tell me how it looks from your side, I'm listening."
- **Don't want to talk:** "That's okay, we don't have to talk about it now. I won't push. Whenever you want, even by text, I'll listen without judging."

**When to get help:** if your teen mentions being pressured, threatened, hurt, or asked for photos → see the help screen (Childline 1098, Cybercrime 1930).

**Sources and check date:** Raise teen survey, 12 teens, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 2 — Stress and feelings

**Topic ID:** `stress`

**The teen's question:** "What do you do when everything feels like too much?"

**Why now:**
81% of Indian school students say exams and results cause them anxiety. Teens in our survey said they want to be heard, not given advice straight away. *(NCERT mental health survey, 2022 · Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Aajkal tumhare mann mein sabse zyada kya chal raha hai? Bas sunne ka mann hai, kuch solve nahi karna."
- Hindi: "आजकल तुम्हारे मन में सबसे ज़्यादा क्या चल रहा है? बस सुनने का मन है, कुछ सुलझाना नहीं है।"
- English: "What's taking up most of your headspace these days? I just want to listen, not fix anything."

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "When things feel heavy, what actually helps, even a little?"
2. "Be honest: is there something I do that makes it worse?"
3. "What's one thing that went okay this week? Even a small one."
4. "How do you know when stress is starting to pile up?"
5. "Who do you talk to when something's bugging you?"
6. "If you had a free afternoon, what would you do with it?"
7. "When you tell me stuff, do you want me to just listen, or help you think it through?"
8. "What's the toughest part of your day right now?"
9. "Is there something you haven't told me because you thought I'd get upset?"
10. "Want to do something together this week, just to switch off? You pick."

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "That sounds really hard. I'm glad you told me."
3. "You don't have to carry this alone. I'm right here."
4. "Your marks don't decide how much we love you."
5. "It's okay to take a break. Come, let's go for a walk."
6. "I felt like this too at your age. You're not alone in this."

**Avoid:**
1. "Just don't think about it" or "Cheer up!" (It tells them their feelings don't count.)
2. Comparing them with siblings, cousins or other kids. (It adds pressure, not comfort.)
3. Jumping straight to advice. (Most teens want to be heard first.)
4. "We never had all this stress in our time." (It brushes off what they're feeling today.)
5. "After all the money we've spent on your coaching…" (Guilt adds pressure, not motivation.)
6. "Only your marks matter right now." (They start to believe their worth is a number.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. We can just sit here for a bit."
- **Get upset:** "I'm sorry, I'm not trying to push. I'm on your side, always."
- **Disagree:** "You know your life better than I do. Help me understand it."
- **Don't want to talk:** "That's okay, you don't have to talk. I'm right here. If you like, we can just sit together, or go for a walk later."

**When to get help:** if for weeks your teen isn't eating or sleeping properly, pulls away from everyone, or says life feels pointless → Tele-MANAS 14416 (free, 24×7). If they talk about hurting themselves → 112 now, and stay with them.

**Sources and check date:** NCERT student mental health survey, 2022 · Raise teen survey, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 3 — Friends and peer pressure

**Topic ID:** `friends`

**The teen's question:** "How do you say no to friends without losing them?"

**Why now:**
10 of 12 teens in our survey learn mainly from friends, and 7 of 12 want parents to talk more about friends. They want questions about their world, not rules. *(Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tumhare group mein aajkal sab kis cheez ke peeche pade hain? Kya trend chal raha hai?"
- Hindi: "तुम्हारे ग्रुप में आजकल सब किस चीज़ के पीछे पड़े हैं? क्या ट्रेंड चल रहा है?"
- English: "What's everyone in your group into these days? What's the big thing right now?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "Has anyone in your group ever been pushed into something they didn't really want to do?"
2. "What makes someone a really good friend, for you?"
3. "Who in your group makes you laugh the most?"
4. "What do you all love doing together these days?"
5. "Have you ever seen a friend stand up for someone? What happened?"
6. "When everyone wants different things, how does your group decide?"
7. "What's a way to say no that still works with your friends?"
8. "Is there a friend you miss, or someone you've drifted away from?"
9. "What do people your age feel the most pressure about, honestly?"
10. "If you ever need a way out of something, would it help to just blame me?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "Saying no to friends is hard. Honestly, adults struggle with it too."
3. "Need an excuse to leave? Blame me. Just say 'my parents won't allow it'."
4. "I trust you to choose good friends. I just like hearing about them."
5. "Your friends are always welcome at home."
6. "If a friend ever pushes you into something, call me. I'll come, no questions asked."

**Avoid:**
1. "Those friends are a bad influence." (They'll defend their friends, and stop telling you about them.)
2. Rapid-fire questions: who, where, how late. (It feels like checking up, not caring.)
3. "When I was your age…" lectures. (They tune out after the first line.)
4. "Why can't you make friends with the toppers?" (It judges their friends by marks, and they stop telling you about them.)
5. Calling their friends' parents behind their back. (If they find out, they'll stop trusting you, and so might their friends.)
6. "Girls and boys shouldn't be friends." (It makes normal friendships feel shameful, so they hide them.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No pressure. I was just curious about your world."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not judging your friends. I just like knowing what your days are like."
- **Disagree:** "Fair enough. What am I missing?"
- **Don't want to talk:** "Fair enough, I won't push. And if you ever need help saying no to someone, you can always use me as your excuse."

**When to get help:** if your teen is being bullied, threatened, or pushed into alcohol, drugs or anything risky → see the help screen (Childline 1098; Cybercrime 1930 if it's online).

**Sources and check date:** Raise teen survey, 12 teens, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 4 — Online safety (photo blackmail, fake images, bullying)

**Topic ID:** `online-safety`

**The teen's question:** "What would you do if someone online asked for a photo?"

**Why now:**
45% of Indian children who faced cyberbullying hid it from their parents. AI apps can now turn any normal photo into a fake one, and there have been cases in Indian colleges (IIIT Raipur, 2025). *(McAfee, 2022 · OECD AI incident report, 2025)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tum log online sabse zyada kahan time spend karte ho? Mujhe bhi samjhao, main thoda peeche hoon."
- Hindi: "तुम लोग ऑनलाइन सबसे ज़्यादा कहाँ समय बिताते हो? मुझे भी समझाओ, मैं थोड़ा पीछे हूँ।"
- English: "Where do you and your friends spend most of your time online? Teach me, I'm a bit behind."

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "Have you heard of anyone getting a weird message or a photo threat? What did they do?"
2. "If that happened to a friend, what would you tell them?"
3. "Which apps or games are you all into right now?"
4. "Seen anything funny online this week? Show me!"
5. "How do you spot a fake account? I genuinely don't know."
6. "What do you do when a stranger sends you a request?"
7. "What would you do if someone online got really rude with you?"
8. "Has anything online ever made you or a friend feel uncomfortable?"
9. "What do parents get totally wrong about the internet?"
10. "Is there a game or creator you could teach me about? I'm so behind!"

**Say:**
1. "If anyone ever threatens you with a photo, come straight to me. You won't be in trouble." (the safety promise)
2. "If someone threatens you, it's never your fault. Never."
3. "Even a fake photo can be reported and taken down. There's real help for this."
4. "You can show me anything that worries you online. I'll stay calm, I promise."
5. "Everyone makes mistakes online, adults too. What matters is telling someone."
6. "If someone online ever scares you, we'll handle it together."

**Avoid:**
1. "Just don't post photos, then nothing will happen." (Fake images can be made from any normal photo.)
2. Threatening to take the phone away. (Teens hide problems when they're scared of losing it.)
3. Checking their phone secretly. (If they find out, trust is hard to win back.)
4. "This phone has spoiled you." (Blaming the phone shuts down the talk about what's actually happening on it.)
5. "Delete everything and don't tell anyone." (Deleting removes proof, and silence leaves them alone with it.)
6. "What will people think if this gets out?" (Fear of shame stops children from asking for help.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. I just want you to know my door's always open."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not accusing you of anything. I trust you. It's some people online I don't trust."
- **Disagree:** "You probably know more about this than me. Tell me what I've got wrong."
- **Don't want to talk:** "That's okay, we can leave it for now. Just remember: whatever happens online, you can come to me, and you won't be in trouble."

**When to get help:** if your teen is being blackmailed or threatened, or a fake or private image is shared → Cybercrime 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, and Take It Down to remove images. Don't pay, don't delete messages (they're evidence), don't contact the person yourself, and don't blame your teen.

**Sources and check date:** McAfee, Cyberbullying in Plain Sight, India 2022 · OECD AI incident report (IIIT Raipur), 2025 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 5 — Consent and personal safety

**Topic ID:** `consent`

**The teen's question:** "Is it okay to say no, even to someone you know?"

**Why now:**
Most people who harm children are known to them: about 97% of offenders in India's POCSO cases knew the child. Only 6 of 18 parents in our survey have talked about consent. *(NCRB, 2024 · Raise parent survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Kabhi aisa hua hai ki kisi friend ko kuch karne ka mann nahi tha, par woh mana nahi kar paaya? Log aisa kyun karte hain?"
- Hindi: "कभी ऐसा हुआ है कि किसी दोस्त का कुछ करने का मन नहीं था, पर वह मना नहीं कर पाया? लोग ऐसा क्यों करते हैं?"
- English: "Has a friend ever not wanted to do something but couldn't say no? Why do you think people go along with it?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "How can you tell when someone really means yes, and when they just feel they have to?"
2. "If something ever felt wrong, which adults would you feel okay going to?"
3. "What does it look like when someone respects your 'no'?"
4. "Is it okay to say no to a hug, even from a relative? What do you think?"
5. "How do you let a friend know you're not comfortable with something?"
6. "Ever seen someone keep pushing after a no? What happened?"
7. "What makes a secret okay to keep, and what makes it not okay?"
8. "Should we have a secret code word, in case you ever need to leave somewhere fast?"
9. "What would you do if a friend shared something private about someone else?"
10. "How can I make sure you always feel safe saying no to me too?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me, whoever it's about." (the safety promise)
2. "You can say no to anyone, even family, teachers or friends, if something feels wrong. I'll back you."
3. "Other people's no matters too. Asking first is always right."
4. "Your body belongs to you. You decide who can hug or touch you."
5. "If something feels wrong, trust that feeling and come to me."
6. "I'll always believe you, whoever it is about."

**Avoid:**
1. "Strangers are the danger." (Most harm comes from people a child knows.)
2. Blaming questions like "Why were you there?" or "What were you wearing?" (Blame makes children stop telling.)
3. "Respect your elders, do what they say", with no exceptions for safety. (Children need to know they can say no to anyone.)
4. Insisting they hug or sit close to a relative when they don't want to. (It teaches them their "no" doesn't count with family.)
5. "Don't make a big deal, he's family." (Children stay quiet about harm when the family's name comes first.)
6. "Girls have to adjust." (It teaches them that their comfort doesn't matter.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. Just know you can always say no, and always tell me."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not worried about anything you did. I just want you to feel safe."
- **Disagree:** "Tell me more. How do people your age see it?"
- **Don't want to talk:** "That's okay, no pressure at all. If anyone ever makes you uncomfortable, you can tell me any time, and you won't be in trouble."

**When to get help:** if your teen tells you someone touched them, hurt them or made them feel unsafe → stay calm, believe them, and call Childline 1098, or 112 if they're in danger now. Don't confront the person yourself.

**Sources and check date:** NCRB Crime in India 2024, POCSO data · Raise parent survey, 18 parents, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 6 — Body and growing up

**Topic ID:** `body`

**The teen's question:** "Is what's happening to my body normal?"

**Why now:**
Puberty questions are often left to school or friends. In a 2026 Bengaluru study, 52% of mothers had never talked about reproductive health with their daughters, and boys usually get even less guidance. *(Rashmi et al., Bengaluru, 2026 · Likith, Bengaluru, 2024)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "School mein body ke changes ke baare mein kuch padhaya hai? Kuch aisa hai jo unhone theek se nahi samjhaya?"
- Hindi: "स्कूल में शरीर के बदलावों के बारे में कुछ पढ़ाया है? कुछ ऐसा है जो उन्होंने ठीक से नहीं समझाया?"
- English: "Has school taught anything about how bodies change? Anything they didn't explain properly?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "What do people your age wonder about but feel too awkward to ask?"
2. "Where do your friends usually look this stuff up?"
3. "Is there anything about growing up that people joke about but don't really get?"
4. "How do you feel about all the changes at your age? Honestly."
5. "What do ads and shows get wrong about how bodies look?"
6. "What would make it easier to ask me something awkward?"
7. "How are your sleep, food and energy these days?"
8. "Would you rather talk about this with me, or with someone else in the family?"
9. "Is there something you'd rather ask a doctor? That's completely okay."
10. "If you saw something online about bodies that confused you, would you tell me?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for asking me anything." (the safety promise)
2. "Everyone grows at their own pace. There's no 'right' time."
3. "If I don't know the answer, we'll find out together, or ask a doctor."
4. "No question about growing up is too embarrassing to ask me."
5. "Changes like periods, a cracking voice or new hair are just part of growing up. Nothing to hide."
6. "You can talk to me, or to anyone in the family you feel easier with."

**Avoid:**
1. Comments on their weight, skin or looks. (Even small comments stay with them for years.)
2. "We don't talk about these things." (They'll go to the internet instead.)
3. Giving medical advice yourself. (For symptoms or worries, see a doctor.)
4. Treating periods as something to hide or be ashamed of. (It adds shame to something completely normal.)
5. "Boys don't cry, be a man." (It teaches sons to hide what they feel about their bodies and changes.)
6. "Ask your mother about this, not me." (Passing them on tells them the topic is too awkward to talk about.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "Honestly, it's a bit awkward for me too. We can keep it short."
- **Get upset:** "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to embarrass you. I just want you to have real answers."
- **Disagree:** "Okay, tell me what you've heard. Let's work out what's true together."
- **Don't want to talk:** "No problem, it can feel awkward. You can ask me later, write it down for me, or text me, whatever feels easier."

**When to get help:** for any medical worry (pain, very irregular periods, anything that doesn't seem right) → a doctor. If your teen seems very unhappy with their body or stops eating properly → Tele-MANAS 14416.

**Sources and check date:** Rashmi et al., parent-adolescent communication on SRH, Bengaluru, IJCMPH 2026 · Likith, factors influencing parent-adolescent SRH communication, 2024 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 7 — Gender and identity

**Topic ID:** `gender`

**The teen's question:** "Is it okay to be different from what people expect?"

**Why now:**
Teens hear a lot about gender roles and identity, at school, from friends and online, but rarely from home. Since 2018, India's Supreme Court has recognised that who a person loves is part of their dignity, and transgender people have had legal recognition since 2014. Many teens still hear slurs and jokes at school. A calm word at home tells them they are safe, whoever they are. *(Supreme Court of India, Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India, 2018 · NALSA v. Union of India, 2014)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Aaj kal school mein 'ladkon jaisa' ya 'ladkiyon jaisa' hone ke baare mein log kya bolte hain?"
- Hindi: "आजकल स्कूल में 'लड़कों जैसा' या 'लड़कियों जैसा' होने के बारे में लोग क्या बोलते हैं?"
- English: "What do people at school say these days about acting 'like a boy' or 'like a girl'?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "Do you think boys and girls are expected to behave differently? Is that fair?"
2. "Have you heard anyone being teased for not being 'manly' or 'girly' enough?"
3. "What do your friends think about people who are gay, or trans?"
4. "Is there anything about this you've wondered about but felt odd asking?"
5. "What did you think when you saw something about this online or in a show?"
6. "If a friend told you something big about who they are, what would you say?"
7. "Do you ever feel boxed in by what people expect from you?"
8. "Which jokes at school go too far, in your opinion?"
9. "Is there anything we say at home that makes this harder to talk about?"
10. "What would you want me to understand about people your age and this topic?"

**Say:**
1. "Whatever you tell me, you won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "Nothing you could tell me about yourself would make me love you less."
3. "I'm still learning about this myself. You can teach me too."
4. "Everyone deserves respect, whoever they are and whoever they love."
5. "You don't have to have everything figured out. Take your time."
6. "If anyone ever teases or troubles you, come to me. I'll be on your side."

**Avoid:**
1. Jokes or slurs about gay or trans people, even casually. (Your child hears them and learns what's safe to share with you.)
2. "It's just a phase." (It tells them you won't take them seriously.)
3. "What will people say?" ("Log kya kahenge?") (They hear that the family's image matters more than they do.)
4. "Boys should be strong, girls should adjust." (It boxes them in, and they stop sharing how they really feel.)
5. Asking "Are you gay?" out of the blue, or checking their phone to find out. (It feels like an interrogation and breaks trust.)
6. Taking them to a doctor or baba to "fix" them. (Being gay or trans is not an illness; it hurts and breaks trust.)

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. I just wanted you to know you can talk to me about anything, any time."
- **Get upset:** "I'm sorry, I didn't mean to make it uncomfortable. I asked because I care, not to judge."
- **Disagree:** "Fair enough. Tell me how you see it. I'd really like to understand."
- **Don't want to talk:** "No problem, I won't push. If you ever want to talk, whenever or however, even by text, I'm here."

**When to get help:** if your teen is being bullied or harassed for who they are → talk to their school, and call Childline 1098. If they seem very low, withdrawn, or talk about hurting themselves → Tele-MANAS 14416, or 112 in an emergency.

**Sources and check date:** Supreme Court of India, Navtej Singh Johar v. Union of India, 2018 · Supreme Court of India, NALSA v. Union of India, 2014 · Checked on 29 Oct 2026

## Card 8 — Adult content and 18+ shows

**Topic ID:** `adult`

**The teen's question:** "Is what they show in films and web series real?"

**Why now:**
5 of 12 teens in our survey named web series and films as a main place they learn about growing up and relationships; only 1 of 12 named parents. In a large US study, 73% of teens had already seen adult content online, over half by age 13. A calm talk at home helps them tell what's real from what's shown. *(Raise teen survey, Oct 2026 · Common Sense Media, 2023)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Aaj kal ki web series mein relationships jaise dikhate hain, kya tumhe lagta hai real life mein bhi aisa hota hai?"
- Hindi: "आजकल की वेब सीरीज़ में रिश्ते जैसे दिखाते हैं, क्या तुम्हें लगता है असल ज़िंदगी में भी ऐसा होता है?"
- English: "The way web series show relationships these days, do you think real life is like that?"

**Gentle questions to ask (10):**
1. "Which shows are people in your class watching these days?"
2. "What do shows get wrong about how people treat each other?"
3. "Have you ever seen something on a screen that felt weird or uncomfortable?"
4. "Do friends ever share videos or clips that you didn't want to see?"
