# Raise — Base Talk Cards (DRAFT for Aayushi to edit)

Status: **first draft by Claude, 25 Oct 2026.** Not yet reviewed by Aayushi or an expert advisor.

## How to edit this file

- Change any wording directly. Keep the headings as they are, so the app can read each part.
- Things in **[square brackets]** are notes for you. Delete or answer them.
- The **first line** is written in all three languages. Everything else is in English. The AI translates it into the parent's chosen language, and personalises it, when it writes each family's card.
- The **safety promise** and **when to get help** stay on every card. The AI is never allowed to change or remove them.

## How the AI personalises each base card

| Setup answer | What changes |
| --- | --- |
| Parent's role | Fathers get a lighter, side-by-side opener and extra reassurance. Mothers get help with words |
| Teen's age band | 13–15: talk about friends and "people your age". 16–18: can be more direct about the teen's own life |
| Teen's gender | Examples and risks that fit (e.g. boys: photo blackmail; girls: fake images, harassment) |
| Language | Hinglish (default), Hindi or English |
| Family comfort level | Gentle or direct wording |
| What the parent wants to talk about | The "why now" and follow-ups lean towards it |

The AI only rewords. It never adds new facts, numbers, advice or help numbers. Those come only from this file.

## Fixed on every card

**Safety promise:** "Whatever you tell me, you won't be in trouble for telling me. We'll figure it out together."

**Help, always one tap away:** Emergency 112 · Tele-MANAS 14416 · Childline 1098 · Cybercrime 1930 / cybercrime.gov.in

**Best moments to suggest (in this order):** a walk · late evening when it's quiet · cooking or chores together · a meal · in the car

## Card 1 — Relationships and crushes

**Topic ID:** `relationships`

**The teen's question (how the topic is shown):** "How do you know if someone is good for you?"

**Why now:**
10 of 12 teens in our survey wish their parents talked about relationships more, but only 1 of 12 learns mainly from parents. Friends and Instagram fill the gap. *(Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tumhare friends mein aajkal kisi ko koi pasand hai? Log kaise pata karte hain?"
- Hindi: "तुम्हारे दोस्तों में आजकल किसी को कोई पसंद है? लोग कैसे पता करते हैं?"
- English: "Do any of your friends like someone these days? How do people figure it out?"

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "What do you think makes someone good for you?"
2. "A lot of what we see in shows and online isn't how real relationships work. What feels fake to you?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "It's normal to like someone. I liked people at your age too."
3. "Real relationships aren't like what's shown in films or online. You can always ask me what's real."

**Avoid:**
1. "You're too young for this."
2. "Who is it? Tell me their name."
3. Jokes or teasing about crushes, even kind ones.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No rush. We can talk another time. I'm here whenever."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not checking up on you. I just want you to be able to come to me."
- **Disagree:** "That's fair. Tell me how it looks from your side."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. Just know you can ask me anything, any time. No questions asked."

**When to get help:** if your teen mentions being pressured, threatened, hurt, or asked for photos → see the help screen (Childline 1098, Cybercrime 1930).

**Sources and check date:** Raise teen survey, 12 teens, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 2 — Stress and feelings

**Topic ID:** `stress`

**The teen's question:** "What do you do when everything feels like too much?"

**Why now:**
81% of Indian school students say exams and results cause them anxiety. Teens in our survey said they want to be heard, not given advice straight away. *(NCERT mental health survey, 2022 · Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Aajkal tumhare mann mein sabse zyada kya chal raha hai? Bas sunne ka mann hai, kuch solve nahi karna."
- Hindi: "आजकल तुम्हारे मन में सबसे ज़्यादा क्या चल रहा है? बस सुनने का मन है, कुछ सुलझाना नहीं है।"
- English: "What's taking up most of your headspace these days? I just want to listen, not fix anything."

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "What helps you feel a bit better when it gets heavy?"
2. "Is there anything I do that makes it harder?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "That sounds really hard. Thank you for telling me."
3. "You don't have to handle this alone."

**Avoid:**
1. "Just don't think about it" or "cheer up".
2. Comparing them with siblings, cousins or other kids.
3. Jumping straight to advice or solutions.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. We can just sit for a bit."
- **Get upset:** "I'm sorry. I'm not trying to push. I'm on your side."
- **Disagree:** "You know your life better than I do. Help me understand."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. If you ever want to talk, even at midnight, I'm here."

**When to get help:** if for weeks your teen isn't eating or sleeping properly, pulls away from everyone, or says life feels pointless → Tele-MANAS 14416 (free, 24×7). If they talk about hurting themselves → 112 now, and stay with them.

**Sources and check date:** NCERT student mental health survey, 2022 · Raise teen survey, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 3 — Friends and peer pressure

**Topic ID:** `friends`

**The teen's question:** "How do you say no to friends without losing them?"

**Why now:**
10 of 12 teens in our survey learn mainly from friends, and 7 of 12 want parents to talk more about friends. They want questions about their world, not rules. *(Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tumhare group mein aajkal sab kis cheez ke peeche pade hain? Kya trend chal raha hai?"
- Hindi: "तुम्हारे ग्रुप में आजकल सब किस चीज़ के पीछे पड़े हैं? क्या ट्रेंड चल रहा है?"
- English: "What's everyone in your group into these days? What's the big thing right now?"

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "Has anyone in your group ever been pushed into something they didn't want to do?"
2. "What do you think makes someone a good friend?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me." (the safety promise)
2. "Saying no to a friend is hard. Even adults find it hard."
3. "If you ever need a way out, you can blame me. Say 'my parents won't allow it'."

**Avoid:**
1. "Those friends are a bad influence."
2. Questions that sound like an interrogation (who, where, how late).
3. "When I was your age…" lectures.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No pressure. I was just curious about your world."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not judging your friends. I just want to know what your days are like."
- **Disagree:** "Fair enough. What am I missing?"
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. My offer stands: you can always use me as your excuse."

**When to get help:** if your teen is being bullied, threatened, or pushed into alcohol, drugs or anything risky → see the help screen (Childline 1098; Cybercrime 1930 if it's online).

**Sources and check date:** Raise teen survey, 12 teens, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 4 — Online safety (photo blackmail, fake images, bullying)

**Topic ID:** `online-safety`

**The teen's question:** "What would you do if someone online asked for a photo?"

**Why now:**
45% of Indian children who faced cyberbullying hid it from their parents. AI apps can now turn any normal photo into a fake one, and there have been cases in Indian colleges (IIIT Raipur, 2025). *(McAfee, 2022 · OECD AI incident report, 2025)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Tum log online sabse zyada kahan time spend karte ho? Mujhe bhi samjhao, main thoda peeche hoon."
- Hindi: "तुम लोग ऑनलाइन सबसे ज़्यादा कहाँ समय बिताते हो? मुझे भी समझाओ, मैं थोड़ा पीछे हूँ।"
- English: "Where do you and your friends spend most of your time online? Teach me, I'm a bit behind."

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "Have you heard of anyone getting a weird message or a photo threat? What did they do?"
2. "If something like that happened to a friend, what would you tell them?"

**Say:**
1. "If anyone ever threatens you with a photo, come straight to me. You won't be in trouble." (the safety promise)
2. "It's never the fault of the person being threatened."
3. "Even a fake photo can be reported and taken down. There's help for this."

**Avoid:**
1. "Just don't post photos, then nothing will happen." (Fake images can be made from any normal photo.)
2. Threatening to take the phone away. Teens hide problems when they fear losing it.
3. Checking their phone secretly.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. I just want you to know the door's open."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not accusing you of anything. I trust you. I just don't trust some people online."
- **Disagree:** "You probably know more about this than me. Tell me what I've got wrong."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. Just remember: whatever happens online, you won't be in trouble for telling me."

**When to get help:** if your teen is being blackmailed or threatened, or a fake or private image is shared → Cybercrime 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in, and Take It Down to remove images. Don't pay, don't delete messages (they're evidence), don't contact the person yourself, and don't blame your teen.

**Sources and check date:** McAfee, Cyberbullying in Plain Sight, India 2022 · OECD AI incident report (IIIT Raipur), 2025 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 5 — Consent and personal safety

**Topic ID:** `consent`

**The teen's question:** "Is it okay to say no, even to someone you know?"

**Why now:**
Most people who harm children are known to them: about 97% of offenders in India's POCSO cases knew the child. Only 6 of 18 parents in our survey have talked about consent. *(NCRB, 2024 · Raise parent survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Kabhi aisa hua hai ki kisi friend ko kuch karne ka mann nahi tha, par woh mana nahi kar paaya? Log aisa kyun karte hain?"
- Hindi: "कभी ऐसा हुआ है कि किसी दोस्त का कुछ करने का मन नहीं था, पर वह मना नहीं कर पाया? लोग ऐसा क्यों करते हैं?"
- English: "Has a friend ever not wanted to do something but couldn't say no? Why do you think people go along with it?"

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "How can you tell when someone really means yes, and when they just feel they have to say it?"
2. "Who are the adults you'd feel okay going to if something felt wrong?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me, whoever it's about." (the safety promise)
2. "You can say no to anyone, even family, teachers or friends, if something feels wrong."
3. "Other people's no counts too. Asking first is always right."

**Avoid:**
1. "Strangers are the danger." (Most harm comes from people a child knows.)
2. Blaming questions like "Why were you there?" or "What were you wearing?"
3. "Respect your elders, do what they say", without exceptions for safety.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "That's okay. I just want you to know you can always say no, and always tell me."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not worried about anything you did. I just want you to feel safe."
- **Disagree:** "Tell me more. How do people your age think about it?"
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. Just remember: if anyone ever makes you uncomfortable, you can tell me, and you won't be in trouble."

**When to get help:** if your teen tells you someone touched them, hurt them or made them feel unsafe → stay calm, believe them, and call Childline 1098, or 112 if they're in danger now. Don't confront the person yourself.

**Sources and check date:** NCRB Crime in India 2024, POCSO data · Raise parent survey, 18 parents, Oct 2026 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 6 — Body and growing up

**Topic ID:** `body`

**The teen's question:** "Is what's happening to my body normal?"

**Why now:**
Puberty questions are often left to school or friends. In a 2026 Bengaluru study, 52% of mothers had never talked about reproductive health with their daughters, and boys usually get even less guidance. *(Rashmi et al., Bengaluru, 2026 · Likith, Bengaluru, 2024)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "School mein body ke changes ke baare mein kuch padhaya hai? Kuch aisa hai jo unhone theek se nahi samjhaya?"
- Hindi: "स्कूल में शरीर के बदलावों के बारे में कुछ पढ़ाया है? कुछ ऐसा है जो उन्होंने ठीक से नहीं समझाया?"
- English: "Has school taught anything about how bodies change? Anything they didn't explain properly?"

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "What do people your age wonder about but feel too awkward to ask?"
2. "Where do your friends usually look up stuff like this?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for asking me anything." (the safety promise)
2. "Everyone's body changes at a different pace. There's no 'right' time."
3. "If I don't know the answer, we'll look it up together, or ask a doctor."

**Avoid:**
1. Comments on their weight, skin or looks.
2. "We don't talk about these things."
3. Giving medical advice yourself. For symptoms or worries, see a doctor.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "It's a bit awkward for me too. We can keep it short."
- **Get upset:** "I'm sorry. I didn't mean to embarrass you. I just want you to have real answers."
- **Disagree:** "Okay, tell me what you've heard. Let's figure out what's true."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. You can always ask me later, or text me if that's easier."

**When to get help:** for any medical worry (pain, very irregular periods, anything that doesn't seem right) → a doctor. If your teen seems very unhappy with their body or stops eating properly → Tele-MANAS 14416.

**Sources and check date:** Rashmi et al., parent-adolescent communication on SRH, Bengaluru, IJCMPH 2026 · Likith, factors influencing parent-adolescent SRH communication, 2024 · Checked on 25 Oct 2026

## Card 7 — Study and future

**Topic ID:** `study`

**The teen's question:** "What would you choose if no one else's opinion mattered?"

**Why now:**
81% of Indian school students say exams and results cause them anxiety. Teens in our survey said they want to be heard before they're given advice. *(NCERT mental health survey, 2022 · Raise teen survey, Oct 2026)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Agar marks aur logon ki baatein na hoti, toh tumhe kya karna achha lagta? Bas jaanna hai, koi pressure nahi."
- Hindi: "अगर नंबर और लोगों की बातें न होतीं, तो तुम्हें क्या करना अच्छा लगता? बस जानना है, कोई दबाव नहीं।"
- English: "If marks and what people say didn't matter, what would you love to do? I just want to know, no pressure."

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "What part of your studies feels heaviest right now?"
2. "How can I help without adding more pressure?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me, even about marks." (the safety promise)
2. "Your marks don't change how much I love you."
3. "There's more than one good path. We'll figure it out together."

**Avoid:**
1. Comparing them with siblings, cousins or toppers.
2. "Do you know how much we spend on your tuition?"
3. Deciding their subjects or career for them.

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No rush. We can talk whenever you're ready."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not here to lecture. I'm on your side."
- **Disagree:** "Okay, tell me your plan. I'd really like to understand it."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. Just know you can talk to me about school any time, even about bad marks."

**When to get help:** if exam stress means your teen can't sleep or eat for weeks, talks about giving up, or says life feels pointless → Tele-MANAS 14416 (free, 24×7). If they talk about hurting themselves → 112 now, and stay with them.

## Card 8 — Screen time

**Topic ID:** `screen-time`

**The teen's question:** "What's a fair phone rule for everyone at home, parents too?"

**Why now:**
The Indian Academy of Pediatrics sets no fixed screen limit for teens. It advises protecting 8 to 9 hours of sleep, screens off an hour before bed, and family rules made together. *(Indian Academy of Pediatrics, 2022)*

**First line:**
- Hinglish: "Ghar mein phone ka ek aisa rule banayein jo sab pe lage, mujh pe bhi? Tumhe kya fair lagega?"
- Hindi: "घर में फ़ोन का एक ऐसा नियम बनाएँ जो सब पर लागू हो, मुझ पर भी? तुम्हें क्या सही लगेगा?"
- English: "Shall we make a phone rule for the whole family, me included? What would feel fair to you?"

**Two follow-up questions:**
1. "When does your phone help you, and when does it get in the way?"
2. "What time should phones go off at night, for all of us?"

**Say:**
1. "You won't be in trouble for telling me about anything you see online." (the safety promise)
2. "These rules are for all of us, me included."
3. "Sleep matters more than screen hours. Let's protect that together."

**Avoid:**
1. Threatening to take the phone away.
2. Checking their phone secretly.
3. Calling them "addicted" or "always on the phone".

**If they react:**
- **Go quiet:** "No rush. Think about it, and tell me what you'd suggest."
- **Get upset:** "I'm not trying to control you. I want rules we both think are fair."
- **Disagree:** "Okay, what would you change? Let's find something that works for both of us."
- **Don't want to talk:** "Okay. Let's pick a time this week to agree on it together."

**When to get help:** if phone or gaming use has pushed out sleep, school and friends for weeks, or your teen seems very distressed without it → the NIMHANS SHUT clinic (Bengaluru) or Tele-MANAS 14416 for advice.

**Sources and check date:** Indian Academy of Pediatrics, screen time guidelines, 2022 · NIMHANS SHUT clinic · Checked on 27 Oct 2026
